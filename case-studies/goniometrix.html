01 Overview

GonioMetrix is a wearable system that measures joint range of motion and provides real-time feedback to improve movement accuracy and consistency.

Traditional goniometers require manual alignment and often another person. The project reframed the problem around independent use, placement clarity, and understandable feedback.

02 Human factors

The challenge was not only sensing angle; it was helping someone set up, move, and interpret a session independently.

What information matters

Current motion, target range, feedback state, and progress need distinct roles.

Physical interaction

Placement and calibration shape whether the measurement can be trusted.

03 Feedback system

Feedback is distributed across channels so a user does not have to stare at a screen while moving.

Visual

The interface shows movement and session history.

Haptic

Vibration makes guidance perceptible at the body.

Auditory

Audio adds another graded signal during movement.

04 Measuring movement

Measurement is treated as an experience with setup and interpretation, not as a number in isolation.

Joint angle

Paired sensor orientation describes the joint’s changing position.

Range of motion

Movement is considered across a session rather than as one static reading.

Thresholds

Target bands translate raw readings into actionable feedback.

05 Wearable experience

The wearable has to maintain placement while leaving the movement itself as unconstrained as possible.

Placement

Repeatable positioning reduces ambiguity between sessions.

Ergonomics

Fit, fastening, and feedback location affect independent use.

Constraints

Prototype size, wiring, drift, and calibration remain visible tradeoffs.

06 System implementation

Sensing, embedded processing, feedback, Bluetooth communication, and the interface operate as one loop.

Dual IMUs

Two sensors capture the relative motion around the joint.

ESP32

The controller processes sensor data and coordinates feedback.

Interface / GUI

A custom interface visualizes movement, logs sessions, and supports progress review.

07 Iteration + tradeoffs

User research shifted the project from measurement alone toward placement consistency, real-time guidance, and feedback that can be understood during motion.

08 Where it landed

The final prototype connects dual-IMU sensing, ESP32 processing, haptic and audio feedback, Bluetooth, and a custom GUI.

My role included research synthesis, concept development, GUI design and implementation, and the final visual presentation.

09 What I’d validate next

Next steps would improve IMU calibration, reduce wearable size, extend the movement set, and compare target consistency with and without feedback.
