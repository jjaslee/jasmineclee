01 Overview

I led visual direction and contributed design execution for Cal Hacks across recruitment, event branding, and hackathon experiences.

The work involved establishing adaptable themes, directing a design team, and keeping digital and physical deliverables coherent across fast event cycles.

02 The constraint

A hackathon identity has to attract attention quickly, communicate clearly, and survive many formats and contributors.

Hackathon context

Recruitment, event information, and on-site experience share one public identity.

Team

A larger design group needs a system that supports distinct contributions.

Time

Event cycles require decisions and handoffs on fixed deadlines.

03 Product direction

The system prioritized visual cohesion, legible messaging, and themes that could adapt across channels.

Priorities

Clarity, consistency, and fast recognition came first.

Scope

Direction covered recruitment, social media, event materials, and hackathon touchpoints.

What we cut

Ideas that could not translate across the required formats were reduced or left out.

04 My design contribution

My contribution combined hands-on asset design with direction for work produced across the team.

Personally designed

I directly designed selected campaign and event materials within the shared system.

Led / directed

I established themes, reviewed work, and guided consistency across deliverables.

05 Collaboration

The design system also acted as a shared decision-making tool across designers and engineers.

Design critique

Reviews aligned individual assets to the current visual direction.

Engineer handoff

Clear assets and behavior notes helped visual ideas survive implementation.

Team alignment

Reusable rules made parallel work easier to reconcile.

06 Shipping

Shipping meant protecting the central idea while reducing detail when time or implementation made that necessary.

Decisions under time pressure

High-impact, reusable elements were resolved before lower-priority variations.

Final product

The released work reflects contributions from the full team.

07 Authorship

Authorship is separated deliberately so the case study does not collapse leadership and team production into one claim.

I designed

Selected visual assets and executions within the system.

I led

Visual direction, themes, critique, and consistency across workstreams.

We built

The broader set of final interfaces, campaigns, and event deliverables as a team.

08 Where it landed

The work produced adaptable visual identities across recruitment and event cycles, with a structure that enabled many contributors to work coherently.

09 What I’d do next

I would document ownership and reusable rules earlier, then create a tighter feedback loop between campaign assets and the implemented event experience.
